The harvest is grown. Then too much of it is lost.

Smallholder farmers in Northern Nigeria lose grain after harvest, in storage and at market. Most help is built for smartphones and English. Most farmers have neither.

  • In the storeWeevils, damp and mould, and rats get into bags kept on the floor or dried on bare ground.
  • At the marketEveryone sells right after harvest, so prices drop when farmers most need cash.
  • In the dataNobody sees where losses are happening until weeks later, after field surveys.

How it works for a farmer

Everything happens in a USSD menu, a text message or a phone call.

  1. Dial the code

    Any phone, any network. Registration asks for a name and local government area, nothing more.

  2. Check before selling

    See today's price at Dawanau market and whether it's rising, so they know when holding is worth it.

  3. Report a problem

    Pick the crop and what's wrong, or call and describe it in Hausa. Storage advice arrives by SMS.

  4. Get paid and matched

    Each report earns airtime. Produce listed for sale is matched to a buyer, who calls the farmer.

Who else uses RUMBU

Buyers and aggregators

Grain merchants, feed mills and processors find supply from hundreds of smallholders without sending agents village to village.

  • Post what you want to buy and your price. Matching farmers are notified by SMS.
  • Browse produce listed today, by crop, bags and LGA.
  • Take a listing in one click and get the farmer's number.

RUMBU takes a small commission on each matched sale.
